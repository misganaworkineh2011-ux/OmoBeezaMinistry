# Omo Beeza Ministry — facts published on the website

## Who we are
Omo Beeza Ministry is a locally established, Christ-centered ministry dedicated to the holistic development of vulnerable children and youth in South Omo, Ethiopia. The ministry began its work on September 28, 2022, in Tsemay (Luqa), with sixteen children from scattered pastoralist settlements of different backgrounds, with the help of missionaries.

The name: "Omo" refers to the local people, while "Beza" means "light" in the Tsemay language — our prayer that the gospel will bring light to those living in darkness.

Motto: "A Transformed Generation for Holistic Change of Community."

## Where we work
Our work is centered in Luqa (Luka), about 63 kilometers from Jinka, in the Tsemay area of South Omo — home to a significant concentration of Tsemay natives alongside a diverse population including the Banna people. Our children are sheltered in Luka and Bodi. Our office is in Jinka, Ethiopia.

## Vision
To raise a generation in South Omo, discipled within their own context and holistically developed to serve as Christ-like, socially responsible agents of change in their communities.

## Mission
Equip the youth of South Omo through holistic education and spiritual discipleship by:
- Equip: educating South Omo's youth through scriptural foundations, academic excellence, and vocational skills.
- Enlighten: nurturing a biblical worldview and a mission-driven heart for unreached peoples.
- Train: developing the next generation of leaders to serve both the Church and the wider community.

## Core values
- Gospel centrality — holistic development for children through discipleship and care for orphans.
- Bible-based ministry — affirming the supremacy of God's Word.
- Partnership — collaborating with churches, ministries, and organizations that share our vision.

## Why we exist
South Omo is rich in cultural diversity and natural resources, yet faces significant socio-economic challenges: limited native leadership, cultural challenges, educational gaps, and poverty. In parts of the region, the traditional belief known as Mingi has labelled some children as cursed, leaving them abandoned or at risk. We believe every child is a gift from God, and we respond with a safe home, daily care, education, and a family where every child is known by name.

## Programs
- Education: structured schooling, tutoring, and vocational training rooted in a Biblical worldview.
- Nutrition & Care: safe shelter, nutritious daily meals, healthcare, and psychological support.
- Joy & Community: daily prayer and worship, play and belonging, spiritual growth and discipleship.

## Numbers
- 33 children sheltered in Luka and Bodi.
- 22 children at Beeza Kindergarten.
- 16 children in the founding cohort (September 2022).
- A 0.7-hectare farm, ploughed and sown with corn with the help of community believers.
